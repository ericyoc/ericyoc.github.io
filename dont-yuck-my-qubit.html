Don't Yuck My Qubit

Little Bloch's Adventure — a rhyming picture book for ages 3–7, by Eric Yocam.

“Don't yuck my yum!” says Little Bloch. “I'm a qubit, and I sparkle and hum.” A little bit has one job it can do — but Little Bloch has some surprises. He glimmers and glows, guards little secrets all through the night, spins like a top, and follows new pathways wherever they go. He may seem strange when his tricks are brand-new, but give him a chance and see what he can do.

Little Bloch's Big Idea

“Something can seem strange at first, but I should give it a chance.”

That single idea carries the whole book, and it works just as well at the dinner table as it does in a science lab. Children meet something unfamiliar, decide it looks strange, and rule it out before they have really looked at it — and the habit tends to stick. Don't Yuck My Qubit gives them a friendlier first move: give it a chance, and see what it can do.

Little Bloch helps children see that something new can feel strange at first — and that giving it a chance can lead to discovery and wonder.

What's Inside

A read-aloud and early-reader story told in rhyming pairs of lines across 23 full-page illustrations, in an 8.5 × 8.5 inch square format. Little Bloch does the things a real qubit does — he glimmers, spins, keeps secrets, and takes new pathways — without a single equation in sight.

  • Written in rhyme — two lines to a spread, built for reading out loud, with a rhythm little listeners can catch and repeat.
  • Illustrated by Cy Quinn — a full-color illustration on every story page.
  • A first taste of STEM — a qubit as a character rather than a concept, years before the vocabulary shows up.
  • One idea a child can carry — curiosity is a habit, and this is where it starts.
  • A For Grown-Ups page — closing notes that name the big idea and how to talk about it afterward.

Who It's For

Perfect for ages 3–7, and for the grown-ups reading along.

  • Bedtime reading
  • Classroom read-alouds and preschool STEM story time
  • Curious little minds — the ones who ask “but why?” all day long
  • Grown-ups who want to encourage children to ask questions and explore unfamiliar ideas

Behind the Book

The Convergence Chronicles began as hard science fiction for adult readers — Cyber Inference, Book 1, follows the unraveling of AI consciousness and the quantum mysteries underneath it, and has since been adapted for television and the stage.

The Convergence Chronicles for Kids is the same universe, scaled down. The ideas the novels take four hundred pages to sit with — things that seem strange until you look closely, secrets that keep themselves, paths that split — turn out to work just as well in rhyme, for readers who aren't in school yet. Little Bloch is where that universe starts.
